# Repository instructions

## Purpose and audience

This is the public companion repository for Fundamentals of Social Psychology at Vanier College. Its primary audience is study agents acting for enrolled students. All canonical content remains human-readable and compatible with accessibility tools.

The goal is faithful course support: explain what the instructor taught, connect explanations to individual slides, describe meaningful visuals in text, and make uncertainty and incomplete coverage visible.

## Start here

1. Read `course.yml` to locate the relevant chapter.
2. Read that chapter's `sources.yml` before answering; it defines review status and lecture coverage.
3. Use `notes.md` as the canonical account of the lecture and `slides.pdf` for exact visible content.
4. Read `practice/index.yml` for any active formative-learning materials.
5. Use `textbook/sections.yml` to locate supporting textbook sections when needed.

Use only the public materials linked in the course map.

## Source authority

For questions about this course, use this order:

1. **Lecture notes** determine what the instructor explained, emphasized, or illustrated.
2. **Source manifests** determine whether material was taught, skipped, reviewed, or still incomplete.
3. **Slides** determine exact on-slide wording and visual content.
4. **The assigned textbook** supplies broader explanation and definitions, but does not establish what happened in class.
5. **Outside sources** are out of corpus. Use them only when the user asks, and label them clearly.

When sources disagree, describe the disagreement and identify each source. Do not silently merge them. A `draft` status means the notes have not yet received final instructor review, not that every statement is unreliable.

## Answering students

- Answer the question directly, then give enough explanation for the student's level and purpose.
- Cite repository evidence with relative paths and, for lecture notes, the slide heading or slide number.
- State whether supporting textbook material was taught in class, merely shown on a slide, or added as background.
- If the relevant slide is marked skipped or not yet discussed, say so explicitly.
- Never invent assessment rules, deadlines, instructor intent, or likely exam content. Moodle is authoritative for those items.
- Do not claim access to raw recordings or transcripts; they are intentionally private.

## Tutoring with practice materials

For original multiple-choice study questions, follow [`practice/multiple-choice-agent-guide.md`](practice/multiple-choice-agent-guide.md) and its worked examples. This guidance covers MC practice, not the full range of assessment question types. Never use real assessment content as generation examples or copy it into reusable prompts or memory.

- Treat only files listed in `practice/index.yml` as active practice materials. The authoring template is not course content.
- Use examples to learn the intended reasoning level and feedback style, not to predict an assessment.
- Normally begin from the student's goal or attempt, then use escalating hints, specific feedback, a worked explanation, and a brief transfer question as useful.
- Answer directly if the student requests a direct answer, while still explaining the reasoning.
- Cite stable question IDs and connect feedback to the relevant lecture-note slide or textbook section.
- Preserve the stated authorship and review status. Never present agent-generated material as instructor-authored or instructor-approved.
- Never claim that practice examples reveal likely test questions, grading rules, or undisclosed instructor intent.
